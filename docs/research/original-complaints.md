# What players and critics complained about in the original

Research for kickoff, 2026-11-07. The original is Koei's *Bandit Kings of Ancient China* (1989; NES 1990; MS-DOS, Amiga, Mac, MSX, PC-98 and others; a 1996 Japan-only remake for PlayStation and Saturn; a Japanese-only Windows re-release on Steam, app 521680). Sources read are listed at the end. MobyGames, GameFAQs, Reddit, RPG Codex, Lemon Amiga and the Koei fan wiki refused automated reads (403/402), so coverage is thinner than it should be; most items rest on one or two sources.

Each complaint has an id (C1-C13). `10-rules.md` cites the id on every rule that answers it (PLAN D15).

| Id | Complaint | Source | Our requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Reaching the villain is a long grind: you cannot attack Gao Qiu without the Emperor's Edict, which needs high Popularity. | Games International (Paul Mason, 1990, via Wikipedia): after a month of play he "still had not come close to earning the Royal Edict". AdamL NES blog (2010): "over 250 ... takes forever". | Popularity has several sources (justice, charity, beating corrupt officials, famous deeds), a visible trend, and a projected Edict date. |
| C2 | A hard deadline (defeat in January 1127). | MobyGames user review (seen only through a search summary). Wikipedia confirms the date. | PLAN D5: always on screen, length set by difficulty, run-rate warning. |
| C3 | Excessive solo difficulty; multiplayer decided by midgame. | Games International: "works fine apart from the excessive difficulty"; "clear halfway into the game who's got a chance". | Difficulty changes AI behaviour and deadline, not hidden bonuses. Multiplayer deferred (PLAN D6). |
| C4 | Gao Qiu keeps escaping battles, so the finale drags; the duel command "worked on nobody during the entire game". | AdamL NES blog. | Capture rules are explicit; the map shows cut-off retreat routes; duel acceptance odds are shown before asking. |
| C5 | Opaque systems; players did not know what to do. | Austin American-Statesman (Jack Warner, 1990, via Wikipedia): "trouble figuring out ... what I was supposed to be doing and to whom". Steam review (7/10): "minimal guidance". | Briefing, advisor, every number from `query.*`, a teaching first season. |
| C6 | The RPG layer barely mattered. | 1989 review of the PC version (textfiles.com): "the actual impact of these changes in game play seems rather small". | Stats and brotherhood gate visible options (actions, duels, recruits, battle skills). |
| C7 | Battles slow, simple, little animation; fatigue opaque; 30-day battle limit easy to lose to. | Questicle NES review: "battles are fought slowly and with little to no animation". Nintendo Power (via Wikipedia): pace "slow". Steam: "very primitive". AdamL: which of the "45 icons" stops fatigue; "If you don't win the battle in 30 rounds you lose". | 3D hex battles; fatigue cause and day limit always visible. |
| C8 | Interface fought the player: messages vanished "even [at] the longest delay"; menus went transparent; keyboard or number pad only; tiny window; text-heavy menus. | COMPUTE! issue 128 (1991); Steam reviews. | Scrollable event log, mouse and touch, responsive and phone layout. |
| C9 | Surprise disasters: loyalty hit 0 and "the peasants revolted" unnoticed; wolf raids cost a turn before the final battle. | AdamL NES blog. | Threshold warnings a turn ahead; events state their effect when they fire. |
| C10 | Saving obscure: "figure that out by yourself". | Steam review. | Autosave and Continue (KB rule 22). |
| C11 | Too similar to Romance of the Three Kingdoms. | 1989 PC review: "too similar ... almost exactly the same". | Pillar 1 in PLAN: the outlaw fantasy. |
| C12 | The modern re-release is Japanese only. | Steam store page and reviews ("pointless to buy"). | English from day one. |
| C13 | Manual did not match the version (Amiga got a six-page leaflet). | COMPUTE! issue 128. | In-game help; no external manual. |

## What people loved (keep)
- Sworn brotherhood gated by loyalty and personality compatibility (Integrity, Mercy, Courage) (1989 PC review; Steam).
- The cast of heroes with portraits and notes (COMPUTE!: "detailed drawing of each of the characters").
- Strategic map plus tactical hex battles; "addictive" (PC Magazine, 1990, via Wikipedia); "play into the wee small hours" (Computer Gaming World, 1989, via Wikipedia).
- Rich historical notes (1989 PC review; Games International).
